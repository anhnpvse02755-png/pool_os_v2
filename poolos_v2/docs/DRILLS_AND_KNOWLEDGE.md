# PART 1: FUNDAMENTALS

## 1.1 The Grip (Cách Cầm Cơ)

### Grip Fundamentals
- **Location**: 15-20cm from butt end
- **Pressure**: Firm but relaxed - like holding a bird
- **Thumb**: Opposes fingers on cue
- **Wrist**: Loose, able to move naturally

### Common Mistakes
- Gripping too tight → Causes tension, affects stroke
- Grip too far back → Less control
- Grip too far forward → Less power

### How to Practice
1. Hold cue with basic grip
2. Squeeze until you feel tension
3. Release 50% of that pressure
4. That's your practice grip

---

## 1.2 The Stance (Tư Thế)

### Basic Stance
- **Front foot**: Pointing toward shot line
- **Back foot**: Perpendicular to front foot
- **Body weight**: 60% on front foot
- **Head**: Low, eyes over cue
- **Bridge hand**: Steady, not rigid

### Stance Types

**Open Stance**: Front foot pointing toward cue ball
- Good for power shots
- Easier for beginners

**Closed Stance**: Front foot pointing away from cue ball
- Better for aiming
- More stable for precision

### Common Mistakes
- Standing too upright → Poor alignment
- Weight on back foot → Lost power
- Head not low enough → Can't see line

---

## 1.3 The Bridge (Giá Đỡ Tay)

### Open Bridge
- Form a "V" with thumb and fingers
- Cue rests in the "V"
- Palm up, fingers spread

### Closed Bridge (Rail Bridge)
- Wrap fingers around cue
- Cue rests on knuckles
- More stable for power shots

### Bridge Distance
- Distance from cue ball determines power
- Closer = softer
- Further = more power
- Standard: 15-25cm from cue ball

### Common Mistakes
- Bridge too short → Overhit
- Bridge too long → Underhit
- Hand not stable → Wobble in stroke

---

## 1.4 The Stroke (Động Tác)

### Stroke Mechanics

**The Pull-Back (Draw back)**
- Smooth, straight pull toward body
- Same speed as forward stroke
- Keep elbow at 90 degrees

**The Delivery (Đẩy cơ)**
- Accelerate through the ball
- Follow through in straight line
- Finish with cue parallel to ground

### Stroke Rhythm
- 60% back, 40% forward
- Or: 50/50 natural rhythm
- Avoid: Jerky, rushed, hesitant

### Common Mistakes
- Jerky stroke → Ball goes off line
- Scooping → Cue comes up
- Stopping at contact → No follow-through

---

## 1.5 Aiming (Ngắm)

### Sight Picture
1. Get behind cue ball
2. Look at object ball
3. See the line from object ball to pocket
4. Align cue along that line

### Contact Points
- **Center ball**: Straight ahead
- **Above center**: Follow
- **Below center**: Draw (backspin)
- **Left/Right**: English (spin)

### Cut Angles
- **0°**: Straight pot
- **30°**: Thick cut
- **45°**: Half ball
- **60°**: Thin cut
- **90°**: Parallel to cushion

### How to Practice Aiming
1. Place cue ball in center
2. Place object ball at various angles
3. Visualize line to pocket
4. Practice without shooting
5. Add stroke after visualization

# PART 2: CUE BALL CONTROL

## 2.1 Stop Shot (Dừng Bi Cái)

### What is Stop Shot
- Cue ball hits object ball and stops
- No forward or backward movement
- Requires center hit with medium power

### Setup
- Cue ball 60cm from object ball
- Object ball 40cm from pocket
- Strike cue ball center

### Common Mistakes
- Hitting too hard → Cue ball rolls forward
- Hitting below center → Cue ball draws back
- Not following through → Dead ball

### Practice Drills

**Drill S-1: Basic Stop Shot**
```
Level: Beginner
Setup: Cue ball 50cm from object ball
```

## 2.2 Follow Shot (Cu Lê/Đẩy Bi)

### What is Follow Shot
- Cue ball continues after contact
- Requires hit above center
- More speed = more follow

### How Much Follow
- **Soft follow**: 20-30cm roll after contact
- **Medium follow**: 50-70cm roll
- **Power follow**: Full table roll

### Setup
- Cue ball 60cm from object ball
- Hit cue ball 1-2cm above center
- Power determines follow distance

### Common Mistakes
- Hitting center → No follow
- Hitting too high → Cue ball jumps
- Too much english → Spoiled follow

## 2.3 Draw Shot (Kéo Bi)

### What is Draw Shot
- Cue ball returns toward shooter after contact
- Requires hit below center
- Creates backward spin

### How to Execute
1. Bridge further from cue ball (20-25cm)
2. Tip contacts cue ball below center
3. Accelerate smoothly through contact
4. Follow through low

### Draw Distance
- **Soft draw**: 20-30cm return
- **Medium draw**: 50-70cm return
- **Power draw**: 100cm+ return

### Common Mistakes
- Jerking the stroke → Cue ball goes left/right
- Hitting too low → Miscue
- Not following through → Dead draw

## 2.4 English (Áp Phê/Spin)

### Types of English

**Left English**
- Spin to the left on cue ball
- Hit cue ball left of center
- Deflects cue ball left after contact

**Right English**
- Spin to the right on cue ball
- Hit cue ball right of center
- Deflects cue ball right after contact

**Top English**
- Forward spin
- Hit cue ball above center
- Makes cue ball roll forward faster

**Bottom English**
- Backspin
- Hit cue ball below center
- Makes cue ball resist forward roll

### When to Use English

**Left/Right English**
- Avoid obstacles
- Create angle after rail contact
- Compensate for deflection

**Top English**
- Increase roll speed
- Get more follow
- Control cue ball on fancy cuts

**Bottom English**
- Draw cue ball back
- Control cue ball on soft shots
- Avoid scratching

### Common Mistakes
- Too much english → Miscue
- English + power → Unpredictable
- English on thin cuts → Cue ball flies off

### Practice Drills

## 3.2 Cut Shots (Cắt Bi)

### What is Cut Shot
- Object ball not in straight line with pocket
- Cue ball must be angled to pot
- Different angles = different difficulties

### Angle Measurement
- **0°**: Straight pot
- **15°**: Very thin cut
- **30°**: Thin cut
- **45°**: Half ball
- **60°**: Thick cut

### Half Ball Theory
- Aim at center of object ball
- Cut affects half of ball visible
- Used for 45° cuts
- Fundamental for all cut shots

# PART 4: POSITION PLAY (Vị Trí)

## 4.1 What is Position Play

Position play means:
- Where the cue ball ends up after a shot
- Planning where to be for the next shot
- Controlling the table

### Natural Angle
- When cue ball and object ball are at 90°
- Object ball goes to pocket
- Cue ball naturally goes to position

### Key Position Principles
1. Always plan position before shooting
2. Use natural angles first
3. Add english only when necessary
4. Leave yourself angles you can handle

---

# PART 5: SAFETY PLAY (An Toàn)

## 5.1 When to Play Safe

**Play safe when:**
- No clear pot available
- You might leave opponent easy shot
- Defense is better than risky offense

**Don't play safe when:**
- You have clear pot
- Easy position available
- Risk is worth taking

---

## 5.2 Types of Safety

### Distance Safety
- Play cue ball to far end of table
- Opponent has long shot
- Low risk of giving away

### Hook Safety
- Play cue ball behind obstacle
- Opponent cannot see path to object ball
- Forces opponent to kick or play safe

### Containing Safety
- Play cue ball to area near opponent's ball
- Limits their options
- Keeps balls clustered

# PART 6: BANK SHOTS (Dội Bi)

## 6.1 Bank Shot Fundamentals

### What is Bank Shot
- Object ball bounces off rail before going into pocket
- Requires understanding of angles
- Speed affects angle of deflection

### Key Principle
- Angle in = Angle out (roughly)
- Hit rail at 30° → Leaves at 30°
- More speed = less angle deviation

# PART 7: BREAKING (Phá Giải)

## 7.1 Break Fundamentals

### Objectives
1. Pocket at least one ball
2. Spread balls for next shot
3. Control cue ball
4. Avoid scratching

### Basic Break
- Power from shoulder and arm
- Bridge about 15cm from cue ball
- Aim at center of rack (slight right for right-handed)
- Follow through fully

# PART 8: MENTAL GAME

## 8.1 Pre-Shot Routine

### Why Routine Matters
- Creates consistency
- Helps focus
- Reduces pressure

### Simple Routine
1. **Assess**: Look at shot, plan position
2. **Visualize**: See ball going in pocket
3. **Approach**: Walk to position
4. **Set**: Stance, bridge, grip
5. **Stroke**: Execute with confidence

### Practice Routine
- Use same routine every time
- Include 3-5 steps
- Don't rush
- Trust your practice

---

## 8.2 Handling Pressure

### Signs of Pressure
- Tight grip
- Rushed shots
- Negative thoughts
- Physical tension

### Coping Strategies
1. **Breathe**: Deep breath before shot
2. **Simplify**: Think about one thing only
3. **Trust**: You've practiced this shot
4. **Reset**: Take time if needed

---

## 8.3 Staying Positive

### After a Miss
- Acknowledge, then forget
- One miss doesn't define you
- Focus on next shot

### After a Win
- Enjoy it briefly
- Don't get overconfident
- Stay focused

### During Losing Streak
- Take a break
- Go back to basics
- Remember why you play

# PART 9: COMMON MISTAKES & FIXES

## 9.1 Aiming Mistakes

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Miss left | Alignment right | Check stance |
| Miss right | Alignment left | Check stance |
| Miss thin | Aim too thick | Use half-ball |
| Miss thick | Aim too thin | Use half-ball |

## 9.2 Cue Ball Control Mistakes

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Cue rolls forward | Hit above center | Practice center hits |
| Cue draws back | Hit below center | Practice tip position |
| Cue deflects | Off-center hit | Straighten stroke |
| Cue jumps | Too high + power | Lower tip, less power |

## 9.3 Mental Mistakes

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Rushing | Pressure | Use routine |
| Overthinking | Fear of miss | Simplify |
| Giving up | Frustration | Reset attitude |
| Overconfident | Success | Stay focused |
